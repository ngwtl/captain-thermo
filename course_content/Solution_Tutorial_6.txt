2) How does ideal solution differ from real solutions? Explain why the Gibbs free
         energy is always decreased when an ideal solution is formed by mixing
         differing elements.

         In an ideal solution, the enthalpy mixing, DHmix is zero. No interaction between
         the components in the solution is assumed and therefore the heat of mixing is
         neither exothermic or endothermic. The Gibbs free energy of mixing is
         represented as:

                        DGmix = DHmix ­ DSmix
         In an ideal solution the enthalpy of mixing, DHmix is 0. Therefore

                        DGmix = ­ DSmix
         For real solution, there is interaction between the components resulting in
         either an exothermic or endothermic reactions.
         The entropy of mixing always increases because it increases the number of
         accessible states. Hence DSmix always increases when mixing components.
         Therefore DGmix always decreases on basis of entropy increases.
3) What does DHmix represents in a real solution? Why the enthalpy of mixing is
         either endothermic or exothermic in a real solution?
         DHmix is the enthalpy of mixing. When different elements are mixed to form a
         solution, the elements are bonded to a different element resulting in a change of
         the bond energy. If the new bond energy is lower then the extra energy is
         released and the reaction is exothermic. Similarly when the energy of the new
         bond is higher, then heat is absorbed and the mixing is endothermic
