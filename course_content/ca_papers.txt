3. 
Why do solid melts when heated above the melting point? 
(20 marks) 
The Gibbs free energy, G is given as 
 
G = H – TS 
 
Where H is the enthalpy and S the entropy. T is the temperature. 
 
It can be seen from the Gibbs free energy equation that the state with the highest 
stability will be that with the best compromise between low enthalpy and high entropy. 
At low temperatures, solid phases are most stable since they have strongest atomic 
bonding and therefore lowest internal energy (enthalpy). At high temperatures, the term 
–TS dominates and phases with more freedom of atom movement, gases and liquids 
become most stable.

1. 
Explain what you understand by an isothermal process and what are the essential 
conditions for this process.  Describe an example of this process in a living organism.  
(20 marks) 
 
An isothermal process is one in which the temperature of the system remains 
constant, i.e. T = 0 during the process. This is achieved by absorbing heat or 
releasing heat during the process. 
 
The essential conditions are (1) the system must be thermally conducting to the 
surrounding and (2) the process must be carried out very slowly (reversible) such that 
there is sufficient time for the heat to be transferred while maintaining a constant 
temperature. 
 
Example of a living organism is the hot blooded mammals where the body 
temperature is kept constant by perspiration of by respiratory action.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

2. 
Explain, briefly the concept of the second law of thermodynamics.  Also explain the 
relationship between entropy, work and efficiency. 
(20 marks)TT 
 
The second law of thermodynamics state that for a spontaneous event to take place, it 
must be accompanied by an increase in the entropy. 
 
A system can lowered its entropy spontaneously by interacting with the surrounding 
or with another system but it must be accompanied by an increase in the entropy of 
the combined systems. 
 
Therefore                        dSsystem + dS surrounding > 0  
 
In the special case where the interaction is perfectly reversible, 
 
                                          dSsystem + dS surrounding = 0 
 
For an isolated system, entropy change in a process is given as 
 
dSsystem = 𝛿Q/T 
 
Therefore for a reversible process, there is no net increase in the combined entropy. 
For a spontaneous process which is irreversible,  
 
dSirreversible  > dSreversible 
 
Increase in the entropy means more heat is required to perform the same amount of 
work. Therefore, irreversible work results in a loss in the efficiency.

D) Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics - This law provides the foundation for the

with a third, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

equilibrium. The statement given in the question is directly related to this 
principle, making E the correct answer.
