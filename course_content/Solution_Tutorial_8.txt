(1) Most metals are extracted from its oxides using C or CO. Explain why it is normally
       carried out at high temperatures?

       Consider the equilibrium partial pressure of oxygen for C ® CO2 and C® CO. In
       order for C to reduce the metal oxides, the standard Gibbs free energy of the oxidation
       of C must be lower that the oxidation of the metal oxides. Take the example of
       oxidation of Cr and Mn. Below 1300°C, these oxidation lines are above the oxidation
       of C. Due to the downward slopes of the oxidation lines of C, at high temperatures
       the oxidation lines of many metals fall below C and hence reduction is possible.

       High temperatures are also employed to accelerate the extraction process.

5) Cu is extracted from the mineral chalcopyrite (CuFeS2). The mineral goes through
       the following FOUR steps below during extraction. The process is guided by the
       given Ellingham diagram for oxide and sulphide. Explain the reasons each step
       described below:

       Step 1 Roasting at about 700°C in air
       2CuFeS2 + O2 ® Cu2S + 2FeS + SO2
       2CuFeS2 + 3O2  2FeO + 2CuS + 2SO2

       Explanation ­ Step 1
       The objective at this stage is to dissociate chalcopyrite into Cu2S, FeS, FeO and S.
       FeO can be easily disposed of in the subsequent Step 2. Examining the Ellingham
       diagram show that below 700°C, Fe forms Fe3O4 which is difficult to remove. Above
       700°C, FeO is more stable than Fe3O4. SO2 (sulphur dioxide), a gas is a by-product
       of oxidation of excess S. Cu2S and FeS is very stable at this temperature. All the
       oxygen will be utilized to form FeO and SO2. SO2 is a gas so it is removed easily.
       The next step is to remove the FeO.

       Step 2 Smelting at about 1200°C
       SiO2 (sand) is added
       FeO + SiO2 ® FeO.SiO2 (slag)
       The slag is removed and the molten material is called Matte. FeS + Cu2S ­ 100%
       Iron and Copper Sulphide

       Explanation ­ Step 2
       FeO combines with SiO2 readily to form and unwanted slag (FeO.SiO2) that floats
       on the molten metal which then can be removed easily. This process has to be carried
       out at 1200°C to ensure FeO and SiO2 are in the molten state.

       Step 3 White Metal Blow - 1200 °C.
       Elimination of FeS by blowing oxygen to oxidse to FeO. O2 and SiO2 (sand) is added
       2FeS + 3O2 ® 2FeO + SO2
       FeO + SiO2 ® FeO.SiO2 (slag)
       The remaining materials is called White Metal ­ no more Fe at this stage.
       Only Cu2S

       Explanation ­ Step 3
       At this stage, the molten metal contains only FeS and Cu2S. Examination of the
       Ellingham diagram shows that at 1200°C, Cu2S is more stable than FeS. By blowing
       oxygen, FeS will dissociate to Fe and S. The blowing of oxygen forms FeO and SO2.
       SiO2 is simultaneously added to form slag with FeO and removed
